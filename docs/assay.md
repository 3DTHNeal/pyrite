# The assay

[All of the guide](README.md)

The assay is Pyrite's fact checker, named for the test that tells gold from fool's gold. `pyrite finish` won't mark a draft ready unless it passes checks that involve no AI. It blocks confidential terms, unsupported claims, numbers not in the fact bank, changed employers, titles or dates, a changed contact line, outside links and unfilled placeholders, and it enforces the page limits. Resumes are also blocked from naming a specialist term that isn't in your approved sources. If a blocked term is true of you, add it to `approved-terms.txt`. It can't catch a true fact twisted into a false impression, so reading each draft still matters.

It also warns, without blocking, about wording that readers now take as machine-written or empty: em dashes, "spearheaded", "leveraged", "results-driven" and the like, and a cover letter over 350 words. Beside the result, the dashboard shows what share of the posting's skill terms the resume uses, and says so when that share is high enough to read as copied (above about 85%) or too low for a skills filter (below about 40%).

- **Confidential names.** A draft may match no pattern under `forbidden`, and no capitalized name listed under "Do not use" in your sources. A pattern under `forbidden` that's a plain name, such as `Bluebird`, also blocks it split by a space or a hyphen and as a plural. The first word of each sentence under "Do not use" is taken for a label, such as "Internal names:", or the start of a description, such as "Customer names", unless it stands alone on its line, is followed by a comma or starts a run of capitalized words, as in "- Bluebird" or "- Osprey Systems". Words in parentheses or quotes are taken for an explanation, so they aren't blocked. Neither is your own name, or a name your sources also state as fact, which `doctor` points out.
- **What counts as a source.** Your approved sources, minus the "Do not use" and "Unsupported" sections of `facts.md`, any `<!-- comments -->`, and lines starting with `#` in `approved-terms.txt`, which approve nothing. The posting never counts, and neither does its title. A resume that opens a bullet with a tool the posting names, and your sources don't, is blocked. A domain or email is a link unless your sources write it, so an employer called Booking.com or a skill called Socket.IO is fine once your sources name it.
- **Numbers.** Digits must appear in your sources, outside your contact details: the digits of your phone number and zip code approve nothing. Numbers written as words from eleven up are blocked unless your sources use the same word, so write them as digits. A multiple such as 8x, a "seven-figure" budget and a change in percentage points are measurements like any other.
- **Disclaimers.** A sentence may name an unsupported claim only to deny it: "I have not led that", or "the posting lists it, and I have not done it". "Led it with no delays", "Never failed an X audit" and "Led X, and I couldn't have done it alone" are still claims.
- **Roles.** Each role heading has to sit under the employer your master resume puts it under, and a line written like a role heading in bold or plain text, such as `**Director** | Madison, WI | 2023`, has to match your record too.
- **Every line is checked.** Only your exact approved contact line is exempt, and an indented heading counts as a heading.
- **Ready means the files on disk passed.** `pyrite finish` is the only thing that marks a draft ready. If a draft's files change afterwards, the dashboard shows **Changed since checked** until you click **Check again** or run `pyrite finish <id>`. A ready draft that fails a later check goes back to blocked.
- **Drafts that keep failing.** After `fix_attempts` failed assays (6 unless you set it under `[run]`), the daily run stops retrying a draft. **Tailor this one first** gives it another set of tries.
- **Passed, with warnings.** A draft that passes but draws a warning a rewrite can clear (a letter naming terms outside your sources or borrowed from the posting, an em dash, filler wording, a long letter, or a resume using more than 85% of the posting's skill terms) gets one more Claude pass from the daily run, told to fix those and nothing else. If that pass fails the assay, the draft that passed goes back, warnings and all. The job's timeline says which happened. Warnings about a file from an older drafter are for you.

What it can still miss: a real number attached to the wrong claim, a number from one to ten written as a word, and an invented skill written in lowercase or opening a sentence without the posting naming it. In a cover letter, a term your sources don't use, such as an invented title, employer or skill, is only a warning, since a letter names the role in its own words; read those warnings. A confidential name, an unsupported claim or an invented number blocks a letter as it does a resume.

## Refreshing a resume

**Refresh resume** on a draft you haven't sent has Claude Code rewrite it for that posting, in the background. The assay and the build run afterward, and any errors go back to Claude, up to three tries. If it still fails, the earlier draft and its documents are restored. A rewrite that passes with a warning it can clear gets one more pass, as in the daily run, though the cover letter isn't this run's to change. Claude gets no tools at all: it hands the whole rewrite back in its closing message, with `notes.md` too if it changed them, and Pyrite writes the files. An answer with no resume in it is asked for again, and after three such answers the run fails and says so. While it runs, the previous draft is kept as `resume.previous.md` in the job's folder. It needs Claude Code installed and signed in. The demo refuses this, since a real run would spend your usage and overwrite the sample draft.
