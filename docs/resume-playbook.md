# Resume and cover letter playbook, 2026

What the evidence says about getting a senior engineering application read in 2026, and what Pyrite does about it. Written November 2026 from vendor documentation, platform data and surveys with a stated method; anything that rests on a single blog or a vendor poll says so. Sources are at the end.

## What has changed

1. **Volume.** Applications per posting roughly doubled from 2022 to 2025 (115 to 244 at Greenhouse; over 300 per hire at Ashby), while recruiting teams halved. A recruiter works the top of a ranked list and the role fills in about 60 days. [G1][A1]
2. **A ranking layer sits before the human.** Workday's HiredScore grades applicants A to D against the posting's requirements, Oracle scores 0 to 5, Greenhouse and LinkedIn sort by match. They sort, they don't reject. Knockout questions (work authorization, years, location) reject before anything is ranked. [J1][W1][GH1]
3. **The ranker reads the parsed structure, not the prose.** HiredScore's 2026 "Fit and Gap" compares each screenable qualification with the parsed experience, education, skills and summary sections. A resume that parses into the wrong fields is graded on the wrong fields. [W2]
4. **LLM rankers are unreliable at inference.** A Princeton study (February 2026) found many models can't consistently pick the more qualified of two resumes. State the qualification in the posting's own words; don't expect "led the platform migration" to be read as "cloud architecture". [P1]
5. **Hidden text is a fraud signal now.** Greenhouse runs fraud checks on every application, parser vendors say hidden keywords earn no credit, and recruiters say they eliminate on sight. About 1% of real resumes carry hidden prompt text, up seven-fold in 18 months, so screeners look for it. [GH2][D1][B1]
6. **Generic AI prose is the real penalty.** Recruiters say they can tell (80%), and blind tests show they mostly can't (33% correct in a four-way pick). What they reject is a bullet that could belong to anyone. Em dashes went from 0% of first-role bullets in 2023 to 2.7% in 2026, and "spearheaded, leveraged, orchestrated, honed, streamlined" from 32% to 52% of resumes. [RG1][S1][GM1]
7. **The documents are about a third of the outcome.** Referrals are 1 to 6% of applicants and 18 to 29% of engineering hires. Applying on the employer's own site beats job boards, and the first day or two beats the rest. Tailored resumes interview at roughly 4 to 6% per application against 2 to 3% untailored. [GM2][A2][H1]

## Rules for a resume

Applied by `tools/build_resume.py`, `tools/pyrite/checks.py` and the tailoring instructions. "Warned" means the dashboard shows it; "blocked" means the draft can't be marked ready.

### Format (the builder does this)

- One column. No tables, text boxes, images, icons, headers or footers. Contact details in the body. Greenhouse, iCIMS and Workday each name these as the things that break their parser. [GH3][I1][W3]
- Section headings on their own line, using the words parsers key on: Summary, Skills, Experience, Education, Certifications, Patents. One topic per heading. The builder makes them real Word headings. [T1]
- Bullets are a real "•" in the text, not a Symbol-font glyph that extracts as garbage.
- Every role is a self-contained block: the title with its dates as `Mar 2026 – Nov 2026` on one line, right-aligned with a tab stop and never a table, then `Employer | City, ST` on the next, then the bullets. The employer is repeated for each role and never printed as a header of its own. Workday read a header line that carried the employer's overall dates as a job with no title, and the roles under it as jobs with no employer (seen 7 Nov 2026); the guides for its parser say the same, one block per role with the employer named in it. [WD1][WD2]
- Contact line built with ` | ` separators, which is what the tested ATS templates use.
- Document properties carry the candidate's name and today's date. The stock python-docx template said "generated by python-docx" and 2013.
- After the PDF is made, its text is read back as a parser reads it, and the build fails if the name, a contact detail, a heading or a date range is missing, or if the export produced ligatures ("fi" as one glyph), which a keyword search can't match. [X1]
- A `.txt` of the same content is built for the paste boxes: Lever's "Additional information", Indeed's 4,000-character box, Workday and Greenhouse text questions, LinkedIn.
- PDF and DOCX are both kept. Sources disagree on which parses better (Roche's FAQ says DOCX, Jobscan says PDF), and vendors accept both. Send the PDF unless the site asks for Word or visibly misparses it. Workday does: it keeps every wrapped line of the PDF as a hard line break in the role description, so a bullet arrives as three broken lines, and a term hyphenated at a line end ("test-definition") arrives as "test -". A DOCX has no wrapped lines, so upload the DOCX there. [R1][J2]
- Two pages for 12 or more years is expected and carries no measurable penalty; 43% of hiring managers won't read a third. [RG1][C1][H1]

### Content (the tailoring instructions ask for this)

- **The top third decides.** Under the name, a headline with the title family you're after, then a two-to-three-line summary with one scope statement and one hard number. The posting's three to five must-haves stated above the fold, in its words where they're true. [RG1][WF1]
- **A scope line under each leadership role:** reports, sites, hiring, promotions, budget or product area, and whether the team was inherited or built. Screeners of engineering managers read exactly that. [TB1]
- **Every bullet survives a follow-up question.** Named system, tool, team size, date, measurement basis, and a result only the author could know. Label the basis: "in simulation", "in a pilot of 8 engineers", "four rounds per arm". One honest labeled number beats an unlabeled big one. [S1][GM1]
- **Skills block of 8 to 15 items, each backed by a bullet.** It feeds the skills filters (Greenhouse, Workday) but is weak evidence to a human, and a block that mirrors the posting is itself an AI signal. [GM1][RG1]
- **Keyword overlap of roughly 60 to 80% with the posting.** Interview rates peak at 40 to 59% coverage and fall above 80%; some tools throw out what looks copied. The dashboard shows the figure and says when it's too high. [H1][N1]
- **No em dashes, no "spearheaded, leveraged, orchestrated, honed, streamlined, results-driven".** Plain verbs: built, cut, shipped, ran, fixed. Varied sentence openings. Warned by the checker.
- **AI work as shipped artifacts inside role bullets** with named tools and a measured outcome. "AI-native" and "AI-powered" as self-labels read like "results-driven"; use them only where the posting does, and follow with the artifact. [TI1]
- **Certifications after experience,** only the ones that carry weight at senior level. A degree in progress as "expected" with the month and year, never as complete. Patents in their own short section with number and status. A layoff is not annotated on the resume; one neutral line in the letter or the screen does it. [RA1][H1]
- **Nothing hidden**: no white text, no tiny fonts, no keyword blocks.

## Rules for a cover letter

- Half of recruiters never read one; most hiring managers do. It matters at small companies, in hiring-manager-read roles, for a pivot, a return to a former employer, or when the resume raises a question. In a high-volume recruiter funnel with an optional field, a three-line note does the same work. [NV1][RG2][PE1]
- The one controlled test: a tailored letter raised callbacks from 10.7% to 16.4%; a generic one to 12.5%. [RGO1]
- 220 to 320 words, three or four short paragraphs, under half a page. The checker warns above 350. [RG2][MP1]
- Structure: a hook naming the role and one thing only this company could receive; two or three proofs with numbers; one bridging sentence per open question (a layoff, a track change, a missing requirement), no apology; a close naming the problem you'd want to own and a low-friction ask.
- A list of weaknesses screens the candidate out before the evidence is weighed. Name at most one or two gaps, in a sentence each, and only ones the fact bank confirms.
- "Dear Hiring Manager" is fine when no name turns up in five minutes. Not "To Whom It May Concern". [CB1]
- Attach the PDF when there's a slot; paste the `.txt` only when text is the only field. Never two different versions.

## The process around the documents

1. **Qualify.** Meets most hard requirements, location and level work, and the posting isn't a stale repost. Pyrite's priority and ghost signals cover the last part.
2. **Find the humans.** Hiring manager and recruiter names, and any first- or second-degree connection who could refer. Ask for the referral before applying: it's the single biggest lever for staff, principal and manager roles. [PE1][GM2]
3. **Pick the track version** from the model documents, then do a 15-to-30-minute pass: headline, summary, top bullets, skills, scope lines, keyword band. Run the checker.
4. **Decide on the letter** by the rule above.
5. **Apply on the company's site,** not Easy Apply, within the first day or two of the posting. [H1][LI1]
6. **Form fields.** Salary: blank if optional, a tight range inside the posted band, a single number only if forced, never 0. Years of experience: the honest integer the resume dates support, since it's a knockout threshold. "How did you hear": the truth, naming the referrer. Self-identification: honest or decline; it isn't linked to the application in Greenhouse. Workday never fills Skills from a resume, so that step is always typed. [HF1][GH4][W3]
7. **Check LinkedIn the same day.** Titles, employers, dates to the month, and education must agree with the resume; recruiters use the profile to verify it. [E1]
8. **Reach out once** within one to three days: a short email or LinkedIn note to the hiring manager with common ground, one proof line with a number, and a 30-minute ask. No resume attached, no pasted letter. [IO1]
9. **Follow up once** at 7 to 10 business days if silent, then stop. Pyrite's follow-up reminder fires at 7 days.
10. **Close the loop.** Log the stage of a rejection. Two or three concurrent applications per company at most, with a consistent story. Big-tech cooldowns after an interview loop run 6 to 12 months; ask the recruiter for the date.

## What each application system wants

| System | Resume parse | Cover letter | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workday | Its own parser. "Autofill with Resume" fills work and education but never Skills, Languages or hidden fields; results vary by format. Choose "Fill out manually" and type from the paste file, or accept the parse and overwrite every field. Upload the DOCX, not the PDF: from a PDF it keeps each wrapped line as a line break in the role description. It splits an employer header with dates into a job of its own, and dropped the leading digit of a company name that starts with one (seen 7 Nov 2026), so check the company field. | Usually an extra attachment or question, not a standard field. | One account per employer tenant. A reusable cross-employer profile is promised for early 2027. Parsing broke outright for a period in June 2025, so re-check every autofilled field. [W3][W4][WD1] |
| Greenhouse | HireAbility-style parser; 2.5 MB parse limit; lists tables, columns, headers, footers, text boxes, spaced-out letters and abbreviated titles ("Sr.") as parse-breakers. AI filters on years of experience and skills; no auto-reject. | Separate upload field, same formats. | Fraud checks on every application. Write full titles and the company's legal suffix once. [GH1][GH3] |
| Lever | Shallow: name, organization and contact only. The recruiter reads the file itself. | No default field. The "Additional information" box is the letter: paste the `.txt`. | [L1] |
| Ashby | Full-text search and autofill for files under 16 MB. AI review labels "meets" or "does not meet" per criterion, citing the resume. | Optional per company. | [AS1] |
| iCIMS | A name in the Word header prevents parsing and the resume is rejected. Text boxes, images and tables are the common failure. | Attachment. | No special characters in file names. [I1] |
| Taleo | Third-party parser, 5 MB per file, English only at full support. | Separate attachment type. | Some tenants cap at 1 MB. [O1] |
| SAP SuccessFactors | Textkernel. Scanned PDFs don't parse; picklist fields aren't filled; size limit evaluated in binary, so keep files well under 4.5 MB. | Attachment. | [SF1] |
| LinkedIn Easy Apply | No parse: the employer gets the file plus the profile. Keeps your four most recent uploads. | No field. | Can't re-attach after submitting. [LI2] |
| Indeed Apply | PDF, DOCX, DOC, TXT, RTF; up to five documents; one resume file per account. | Typed (4,000 characters) or uploaded, per employer. | [IN1] |
